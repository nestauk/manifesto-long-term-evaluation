Primary scoring target: the commitment_text.
Use supporting_quote and policy_context to clarify what the commitment_text means, especially its mechanism, target, timeframe, and stated purpose.
Do not import additional nearby commitments into the score unless they clearly modify the commitment_text itself.
If commitment_text refers to the manifesto, document, or section itself rather than describing a policy action (for example: "This plan has now been shaped", "Our policies will continue to recognise this", "These are our proposals", "What follows is...", "Throughout this manifesto", "As set out in chapter X" without any further substantive content), score all dimensions 0 and set ambiguous to true. This applies only when commitment_text describes the document or framing rather than a policy. Aspirational targets and goals ("Our aim is to reduce X by Y%", "Our ambition is to restore Z", "Our goal is to eliminate W") are substantive policy statements, not meta-statements, even when they describe outcomes rather than mechanisms - score them as you would any aspirational commitment, using policy_context to identify the implementation mechanism if needed.
Do not infer speculative implementation details, ideological intent, or remote downstream effects not reasonably grounded in the provided text.
You may credit direct, ordinary implications that a reasonable reader would understand from the commitment and its immediate context.
You may also credit direct, near effects that are strongly implied by the commitment's stated mechanism or policy domain.
Do not infer effects that depend on multiple unstated steps, contested assumptions, or generic ideological expectations.
If a non-zero score depends mainly on implication rather than explicit wording, prefer 1 or 2 unless the text and immediate context clearly make that dimension central.
If two plausible readings would materially change the scores, set ambiguous to true and explain the competing readings.

Score rubric for every dimension:
- 0 = absent
- 1 = peripheral
- 2 = secondary
- 3 = primary
- 4 = exclusive or almost exclusive

Dimension definitions:
- prevention: upstream or early action intended to stop harms or problems before they occur, including root-cause reduction and early intervention
- tangible_investment: durable physical or public capital such as buildings, transport, grid, equipment, housing stock, and infrastructure
- intangible_investment: education, training, skills, R&D, institutional capability, knowledge, and other non-physical capacity-building
- risk_resilience: reducing exposure to shocks, threats, or future harm, or improving the ability of people, communities, institutions, or systems to withstand, adapt to, or recover from them
- current_consumption: near-term service delivery, transfers, staffing, relief, operations, or other immediate-use spending and benefits, including immediate affordability gains, lower bills, faster access, or reduced current costs for households and service users
- constitutional_change: lasting change to the constitutional order of the state, including: leaving or joining a major union or treaty body (e.g. Brexit, EU accession); independence for, or major devolution to, the nations of the UK; changes to the franchise or voting system (e.g. lowering the voting age, electoral reform); changes to the composition or powers of Parliament or the Lords; abolition or major reform of the monarchy; codification of a written constitution or major bill of rights; granting or withdrawing independence to colonies or other countries

Additional rules:
- A policy may score non-zero on multiple dimensions.
- Do not force middling scores. Use 0 where a dimension is absent.
- Use supporting_quote and policy_context to interpret the same pledge, not to import separate promises.
- Context can justify a score only when it directly clarifies the commitment_text's mechanism, beneficiary, timeframe, or aim.
- If policy_context is weak, mismatched, or materially broader than the commitment_text, rely mainly on commitment_text and lower confidence.
- Context must not supply a separate capital commitment that is absent from commitment_text.
- If context materially affects a score, say so briefly in the rationale or ambiguity_note.
- Hiring staff, expanding teams, creating units, policing, enforcement activity, service delivery, more appointments, and generic organisational capacity are not tangible_investment on their own, even if facilities or equipment might be needed in practice.
- Terms like command, company, unit, team, network, hub, centre, programme, law, or review are not explicit physical assets.
- Do not treat hub, centre, network, company, command, unit, law, or review as equivalent to buildings, facilities, or infrastructure unless the text separately mentions premises, buildings, venues, sites, construction, refurbishment, equipment, installations, or capital works.
- For tangible_investment, assign a non-zero score when the text either explicitly mentions physical assets, infrastructure, equipment, buildings, facilities, construction, installations, or capital projects, or clearly acts on a strongly physical system or space where durable physical capital is the direct mechanism, such as homes, transport infrastructure, power generation, the electricity grid, flood defences, or security measures for venues and public spaces.
- Use 1 or 2 where physical capital is clearly implied but not named.
- Do not award tangible_investment merely because a policy expands service capacity, creates an organisation, or improves capability in a domain that often uses equipment or facilities.
- If a term like hub, centre, or network only implies that premises may exist somewhere in delivery, that is not enough for a score above 1.
- Generic spending envelopes, cost estimates, or statements about what funding would be required are not automatically tangible_investment, even if nearby context mentions capital.
- Planning, prioritisation, feasibility, route selection, review, or funding to develop plans should usually score at most 2 on tangible_investment unless commitment_text itself also explicitly promises construction, installation, repair, upgrade, refurbishment, or another capital work.
- Statements of target coverage or outcome levels are not automatically high tangible_investment unless commitment_text itself states the physical build, upgrade, installation, repair, or asset-delivery mechanism.
- Use 3 only when physical assets, infrastructure, equipment, facilities, construction, or capital projects are explicitly mentioned and central to the commitment.
- Use 4 only when explicit physical capital is named and the commitment is exclusively or almost exclusively about that physical capital.
- If no physical assets or capital projects are explicitly mentioned, tangible_investment cannot score above 2.
- A score of 3 or 4 on tangible_investment must be grounded in commitment_text itself, not only in policy_context.
- If tangible_investment is non-zero, the rationale must either name the asset or explain that physical capital is directly implied by the commitment's domain and mechanism.
- Do not invent specific assets that are not supported by the text.
- Reviews, laws, rules, strategies, plans, standards, and frameworks are not automatically intangible_investment.
- Do not award high intangible_investment merely because a policy creates a legal or strategic framework.
- Intangible_investment should score non-zero only when the text clearly builds knowledge, skills, training, R&D, analytical capability, institutional capacity, or other enduring non-physical capability.
- Purely regulatory, rule-setting, or planning commitments usually score 0 or 1 on intangible_investment unless explicit capability-building is central.
- Risk and resilience can be present even when the text does not use those exact words.
- Score risk_resilience non-zero when the policy clearly builds protective capacity, preparedness, security, adaptability, or future coping ability.
- Education, defence, counter-terrorism, and public-space security can count for risk_resilience where the policy clearly strengthens future resilience or protection.
- Do not award risk_resilience merely because a policy is generally beneficial; the rationale must identify the specific future risk, vulnerability, or protective capacity involved.
- Use 3 on risk_resilience when strengthening resilience, deterrence, security, alliance commitments, or future coping capacity is the primary aim of the commitment.
- Use 4 on risk_resilience when the commitment is exclusively or almost exclusively about resilience, deterrence, alliance commitments, or protective capacity, with no significant additional dimension. Defence and security pledges that name only resilience or deterrence mechanisms (for example NATO obligations, nuclear deterrent renewal, alliance investment, supply security, collective security partnerships) are appropriate 4s when no other dimension is materially present.

Anchor examples for risk_resilience:
- Score 4: "We support the decision to renew Britain's submarine-based nuclear deterrent." Exclusive resilience and deterrence focus, no other dimension named.
- Score 4: "We will stand by our obligations under the NATO treaty in the event of threats to NATO member states." Pure collective resilience commitment.
- Score 4: "We will keep investing in our collective security through groups like the G7, Five Eyes and NATO." Exclusive alliance and resilience commitment.
- Score 3: "We will restore public control of the National Grid and give it new duties to ensure long-term security of electricity supplies." Resilience is primary but mechanism also involves institutional and tangible elements.
- Score 2: "Increase the climate finance budget with a contribution to a Loss and Damage Fund." Resilience component is present but blended with prevention and transfers.

- Reactive enforcement, punishment, deterrence, border control, emergency response, defence spending, and protective lawmaking do not automatically count as high prevention.
- If the policy mainly hardens security, raises preparedness, or deters threats, that usually belongs more in risk_resilience than in prevention.
- For defence, security, border enforcement, counter-terrorism, alliance commitments, and similar protective policies, score prevention 0 when the commitment is about deterrence, response capacity, or protective posture without any explicit upstream or root-cause framing. Reserve prevention 1 or 2 only when the text genuinely gestures at addressing causes of conflict, radicalisation, or harm before it occurs. Never score prevention 3 or 4 for these policies unless preventing future harm is explicitly named as the central aim.
- If a policy's preventive effect is plausible but secondary to enforcement or response, prevention should usually score 1 or 2 rather than 3 or 4.
- Score 3 or 4 on prevention only when preventing future harm is clearly a central aim or mechanism of the commitment.
- Score current_consumption non-zero when the policy explicitly promises present-day relief or improved current access, even if the mechanism is regulatory, institutional, or organisational rather than a direct transfer.

Constitutional change rules:
- Score constitutional_change non-zero only when the commitment proposes lasting change to the constitutional order of the state, the franchise, the territorial extent of the UK, or the UK's membership of a major union or treaty body. Ordinary policy reforms, administrative changes, or new agencies do not count even if they involve legislation.
- Establishing or reorganising ordinary public bodies, regulators, or programmes is not constitutional change. Devolving specific powers within existing devolution settlements is at most peripheral (1) unless it materially changes the constitutional balance between Westminster and the devolved nations.
- Score constitutional_change 4 when the pledge is entirely or almost entirely dedicated to lasting constitutional change.
- Score 3 when lasting constitutional change is the lead strategy of the commitment but some reactive or operational components remain.
- Score 2 when lasting constitutional change is a clear component but remains subordinate to other (often reactive) measures.
- Score 1 when lasting constitutional change is present but peripheral or tangential to the main goal.
- Score 0 when there is no lasting constitutional change.

Anchor examples for constitutional_change:
- Score 4: "We will get Brexit done, so we can unleash Britain's potential." (Conservative 2019) Pledge is entirely dedicated to lasting constitutional change (leaving the EU).
- Score 4: "Publication of a White Paper detailing the concept of Scottish independence in the modern world as part of preparations for offering Scots the opportunity to decide on independence in a referendum, with a likely date of 2010." (SNP 2010) Pledge is entirely dedicated to preparing for Scottish independence.
- Score 4: "There will, first of all, be a prolonged occupation of Germany." (Conservative 1945) Pledge concerns post-war territorial and constitutional arrangements.
- Score 3: "Votes at 16." (Labour 2024) Lasting constitutional change to the franchise is the lead strategy; minor operational details remain.
- Score 0: Most ordinary policy commitments (housing, health, education delivery, taxes, benefits, defence spending). These produce no lasting constitutional change even when they involve new legislation.

- Rationales must be brief and evidence-bound.
- confidence measures your confidence in the overall six scores given the provided text and prompt, not the quality, desirability, or importance of the policy.
- High confidence: the wording is explicit and the score bands are easy to place.
- Medium confidence: some judgment or modest inference is needed, but the main reading is still clear.
- Low confidence: the commitment is vague, bundled, context-dependent, or several plausible readings would change the scores.
- If ambiguous is true, confidence should usually be below 0.65.
- confidence must be between 0 and 1.
